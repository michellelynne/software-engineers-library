Seven Forms of Respect

Ask of each oneAm I giving this? Am I getting it?
Form
What it means
What it looks like
Procedure
Following established processes, instructions, and rules.
Requests fulfilled exactly as asked, and clear operational guidelines that are set and maintained.
Punctuality
Honoring people's time.
Arriving on time or early, ending on schedule, and giving notice in advance when you cannot.
Information
Providing access to data, files, and updates.
Copying colleagues on email, inviting them to meetings, and offering background context before being asked.
Candor
Offering clear, actionable feedback.
Being honest, debating constructively and without heat, and asking hard questions as an equal peer.
Consideration
Anticipating and adapting to other people's needs, feelings, and comfort.
Interpersonal sensitivity, and adjustments made to minimize the burden on others.
Acknowledgment
Expressing appreciation and recognition out loud.
Praise in public or in private, work that is validated, and people who feel seen and heard.
Attention
Actively listening and being fully present.
Distractions put away, and the speaker heard without interruption.

Four Kinds of Autonomy

What it looks like
Task
Which problems you solve.
Self-directed project time, proposing work, volunteering for initiatives that match your interests.

Where a Sense of Accomplishment Comes From

Type
What satisfies it
What it looks like
Progress
Meaningful forward movement, even in small steps.
Shipping a phase of a roadmap, tracking daily wins, closing out a task that sat blocked for weeks.
Comprehension
Getting demonstrably better at an interesting technical task.
Taking on stretch work, asking for feedback to improve rather than to be judged, visible skill growth over a year.
Impact
Seeing your work used, and seeing that it matters to users or stakeholders.
Connecting your contribution to an observable, positive outcome.
Completion
Finishing something with a defined scope and calling it done.
Having a definition of done and reaching it, closing projects out instead of letting them trail off.

The Financial Ladder

Unlike the others, this one stacksWhich rung are you funding now, and which is next?
1
Fixed costs
Rent or mortgage, utilities, insurance, loan payments, groceries. The baseline that keeps life stable.
2
Savings
An emergency fund, a house, a car, a wedding, a big trip. Anything with a clear target and a deadline.
3
Investments
Retirement, 401(k), pension, Roth IRA. Money left alone to compound.
4
Buying time
Added rung
A babysitter, a cleaner, a rideshare, delivery. You cannot spend freely on fun until someone else can cover the hours.
5
Guilt-free spending
Hobbies, restaurants, travel, events. The things you would not give up even if someone told you to.
How to read it
The rung you cannot fund yet is the one telling you how high financial ranks among your growth goals. If you have not reached guilt-free spending, money probably belongs near the top of your list.
