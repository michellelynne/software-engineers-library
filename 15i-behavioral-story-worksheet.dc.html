Behavioral Story Bank

Start with your most recent favorite project, ideally one where you showed leadership or worked as a Senior engineer. The project itself may have been chaotic. You can smooth out the edges when you tell it. The interviewer knows they are only hearing the important parts. Fill this out once per project until you have more stories than you think you need.

Why this works
"People don't want to buy a quarter-inch drill. They want a quarter-inch hole."
Theodore Levitt
01

The Project

1Project name
2What problem did you solve? In words anyone at the company would understand.
3What was your role?
4Who did you work with? Use plain titles: manager, teammate, team lead, stakeholder.
02

What Happened

1Did you get feedback during the design phase?
2Did it change your solution? Why or why not?
3What were the major milestones?
4What blockers did you have to get past?
5Any issues with collaborators?
6Any major pivots from outside forces? A new tax that changed the math, for example.
7Did you distribute tasks?
8Did you mentor a more junior engineer on it?
03

How It Landed

1What is different today because of what you did?
2Any patents granted, or other major accomplishments?
3What did you learn?
4If you started over fresh, what would you do?
5Draw the line from your work to a goal anyone would recognize

It can take a few hops. "I did x, which affected y, which led to z, and in the end it sold more mugs."

04

Write the Story

Now use the details above to write the story where this project showed good judgment. Beginning, middle, end, told in the order it happened. Then write it again for another skill.

1The judgment story
2Which skill is this second story for?
05

Skill Coverage

Name the story you would tell for each skill. Any gap here is a question you are not ready for. Before a specific interview, redo this using the skills in that organization's values or mission statement.
