Emotional Intelligence
Reading and managing the feelings of peers, managers, and stakeholders in hard moments, and describing actions rather than passing judgment on people.

Effect on your team and the people around you, not only on your own tickets.

Beginning, Middle, End

Tell it in orderMost people start at the end, because they already know how it turned out.
Beginning
Set the scene in two sentences. What the product was, in words anyone could follow, and what your role on it was.
Name the cast in plain titles: manager, teammate, team lead, stakeholder, mentee. No internal job titles.
Middle
What you actually did, in the order it happened. The blockers, the feedback you got, and what you changed because of it.
Specific about your behavior, general about the technology. The technical deep dive belongs in another interview.
End
How it landed, what it changed for the business, and what you learned and carried into the next project.
Draw the line to a goal anyone would recognize: more sales, less support, hours saved.
Editing rule
Three points, not five. Cut half the words. Once you have made the point, stop selling it.

Scope Questions and Scale Questions

System design openingAsk a few of each, then get to the boxes.
The prompt
"I have a contractors marketplace app used in hundreds of cities worldwide. Contractors can search for clients and bid on jobs. I want to add a vendor catalog feature. Parts suppliers can upload product catalogs and keep them up to date. How would you design this?"
Scope
the workflow, sometimes called fundamental
Who uploads the catalogs, the suppliers themselves or an admin?
What formats can they upload? CSV, PDF, their own API feed?
What is in a catalog? Names, descriptions, prices, photos?
Does it need real-time availability, or is approximate stock fine?
Do vendors push updates, or do we pull from their inventory?
Can contractors order in the app, or is the catalog reference only?
Is search across all catalogs, or per vendor?
Are prices public, or negotiated when a contractor makes contact?
Scale
the size, sometimes called non-fundamental
How many vendors do we support at launch?
How many SKUs does a vendor carry?
How often does a vendor send an update?
Do vendors specify the region they supply?
How often is each catalog or item updated?
Speed against consistency is the usual question here. In the real world the answer is always both, so ask instead how fast this particular user or service needs an answer. Are they waiting on a page, or on an email?
The hints in this prompt
"Keep them up to date" means this is not a one-time upload. "Hundreds of cities worldwide" means the scale is international. Those are the two real problems.
