Where a Broken Thing Goes

Incoming request
“I finished the style quiz and my results are gone.”
The customer calls or opens a chat
1
Customer Service
First contact for the customer. Solves the simple, frequent things (a failed login), explains how to do a task when someone is confused, and documents anything it cannot solve. Keeps the customer posted with an ETA while the escalation runs.
Example
A homeowner is not seeing their Style Quiz results. They need a number to call or a chat window to ask in.
Escalate
Stops here if: the password reset worked, or the answer was “tap the saved tab.” Most tickets end on this line.
2
On-Call Triage Only at organizations large enough to have a team per dependency
Reads tickets first and decides which team owns each one, then hands it off. No fixing happens here, only routing.
Example
Several people have now called about missing Style Quiz results. Customer Service has escalated it.
Route to the owning team
Stops here if: the ticket belongs to another team’s dependency. It leaves your queue entirely.
3
On-Call Engineer
Decides whether this becomes an incident. Sets priority and visibility: into the current sprint or the backlog, enough detail for the assigned engineer or back for more.
Example
Permissions are correct, but the quiz results API returns nothing. That API is yours, so the ticket lands with your team.
Wide impact, so declare an incident
One user or a small blast radius, so queue it
4
Incident Manager Usually the on-call engineer
Communicates severity and impact to every team, pulls in whoever is needed, and keeps the incident moving until it is resolved. Follows up so everyone affected hears how it ended.
Example
Results are missing for everyone who took the quiz in the last week. Earlier results are fine. The on-call engineer starts an incident.
Team Engineer Anyone on the team, by rotation
Picks up incoming bugs and requests. Have a written rule for when and how tickets get picked up so it is not the same person every time.
Example
One homeowner’s quiz answers never saved. It is a bug on the board, not an incident.
The loop back
Every status update, ETA, and resolution travels back down the same chain to Customer Service, who tells the homeowner. A fix nobody communicates still reads as a broken product.

Where a Request That Is Not Broken Goes

Incoming request
“I need help getting set up,” or “I want the product to do something new.”
Who is asking: one contractor, stuck partway through the automated onboarding tool.
Operations
Configures new client accounts by hand. Technical expertise, but not building product features.
Example
A contractor cannot get their catalog connected through the self-serve tool. They hand over their details and Operations sets it up.
Who is asking: an enterprise client moving off their own system.
Sales Engineer Mostly enterprise products
Embedded with the new client. Reviews their current tech, is their first point of contact through the transition, and escalates real problems to product engineering.
Example
A large contracting organization is replacing its internal product and website with Renovation Revolution. A dedicated person makes the move smoother and keeps them from churning.
Who is asking: a customer who wants the product to do something it does not do yet.
Team Engineer Via Customer Service
Feature requests arrive as tickets on the same board as bugs. They get prioritized against the roadmap rather than answered at the front desk.
Example
Someone asks Customer Service to let them print their Style Quiz results. Today results can only be shared inside the app.
The one rule both charts share
Not every organization has every one of these roles. Your job is to know which name on the chart the request lands on at your organization, and who picks it up when that person is out.
