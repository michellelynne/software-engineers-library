Starting a Mentorship

Mentoring your first peer can be daunting. Use this worksheet to set up the relationship for success. Plan a strong first meeting, then keep the feedback that follows clear and kind.

01

Schedule Your First Meeting

Your first meeting sets the tone of the mentoring relationship. Introduce yourself, explain why you're here, and discuss how the relationship will work. Decide if you will meet regularly or as needed. Have the mentee schedule any meetings. If they are not interested in your advice, it's a waste of time for both of you. Draft what you'll say below.

Mentee
Proposed first meeting
1Who you are
Your role, background, and the experience that makes you a good mentor for them.
2Why you're mentoring them
Your manager wanted you to help them get up to speed on the code and the culture. Make it clear you're here for whatever they need.
3How it will go
Recurring or ad-hoc meetings. They can ask anytime on team chat. Expect periodic feedback.
02

Giving Feedback

Whether the feedback is good or bad, it shouldn't wait for meetings. Feedback should be timely and actionable. Focus on their actions and the results. If positive, you can ask if they want public recognition. If negative, ask them how they would like to solve the issue. Agree on a timeline for a change and schedule a follow-up. Use the structure below to help you write it out.

1The action: “When you…”
When you interrupted Suzie in the meeting…
2The consequence: “When you do that…”
…peers feel unheard and can choose not to work with you.
3Ask how they'd change it, then commit to a follow-up
“How would you handle it next time?” Agree on a time period to try it, and schedule a follow-up meeting.
Don't mind-read
Avoid “I know you thought she was going on too long.” You don't actually know what is going on in anyone's mind. Stick to what happened and what to do next.
Example feedback
“When you submit a pull request without adequate testing, we often find bugs. How can you make better use of the reviewer's time?”
