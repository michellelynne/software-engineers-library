3 Tickets

We are only trying to confirm whether people would use this. So one thing we don’t need is onboarding. We can do that manually and seed sample data, so users see the best features instead of getting stuck gathering their own information together first. For every ticket ask: does a homeowner need it to send a project to a contractor, or does a contractor need it to find that project and get in touch? If not, it goes on the cut list below.

4 Cut from the MVP

Naming what you are not building is as useful as the ticket list. These are real parts of the case study that do not help prove the hypothesis yet.

Contractor OnboardingWe sign up the first contractors ourselves and load their licensing and service area by hand.
Room CaptureThe 3D scan is the most expensive part of the design. The MVP will just use photos.
Project BiddingContractors will contact homeowners manually.
In-App MessagingUsers can use email to contact each other. Later, we’ll keep them in the app with in-app messaging.
Style QuizTaste matching only matters once people are picking products. Nothing in the hypothesis depends on it.
Order & Delivery TrackingEverything after a contractor takes on a project, including Purchase Review and Supplier Deals, waits for a second experiment.
All vendor pagesProduct Catalog, Featured Products, and Homeowner Interest Insights need a marketplace we don’t have users for yet.
 
 

5 MVP Estimate

Backend and infrastructure hours divide by the backend capacity, frontend hours divide by the frontend capacity. The two tracks run in parallel, so start with the slower one, then multiply it by 3 and round up to whole weeks. That padding covers meetings, reviews, bugs, and the unknowns.
