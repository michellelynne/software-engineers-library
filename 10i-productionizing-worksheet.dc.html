Productionizing

Being able to determine whether an app is ready for production and how to get it there is a key skill for a Senior Engineer. Susan J. Fowler defines a production-ready service as one that is stable, reliable, scalable, fault-tolerant, performant, monitored, documented, and prepared for catastrophe (Production-Ready Microservices, O'Reilly, 2016). Since she already made us that excellent breakdown, we'll use it to analyze any app you're currently working on.

This worksheet works on multiple levels. You are testing your app, but also your skills. For each category, you need to be able to make a judgment call and know what improvement looks like. Being perfect in every way is basically impossible. Software is built to meet organizational goals, not to be technically perfect. Higher-traffic services do need to be better, since it will be easier to see the fault lines. Software also degrades over time, so it could be an 8 a year ago and a 6 today.

The value of this worksheet lies in identifying gaps and prioritizing improvements, both for the app and for your skills. I have included a mini further reading list at the end for any section you want to take a deeper dive into.

Almost nothing scores a ten across the board, and it does not need to. Zero is nothing in place, five is partly there, and ten is fully handled and verified. The numbers in between are yours to judge. Be honest rather than generous.

For each section:

  • Read the three definitions
  • Mark where your application sits today
  • Say why you picked that number
  • Write down the next step
  • Note the impact on the organization, both if you make the change and if you do not
