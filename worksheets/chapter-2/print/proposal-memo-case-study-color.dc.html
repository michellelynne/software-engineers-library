Proposal Memo

Renovation Revolution

Senior engineers are proactive. Proposing a new idea is a win for you, even when it is not approved, because it demonstrates your initiative. A memo is also a great way to learn:

  • •your organization's memo standards
  • •how leadership reacts to new ideas
  • •how your team gives feedback
  • •how to connect your goals to leadership's goals

This is filled in with answers from the Renovation Revolution case study.

1

Audience & Goal

The audience is usually your manager, but they may share it upward or ask for stakeholder feedback. Name a goal of theirs that this memo will address.

Who is this memo for?
Which of their goals does this memo address?
My engineering manager
Grow the customer base with a new product line
The VP of Product
Diversify how the company reaches customers
2

Blockers

What is preventing that goal from being reached today.

•Homeowners struggle to find and compare qualified contractors for a renovation.
•Contractors have no easy way to discover relevant local projects to bid on.
•Estimating labor hours and parts cost for a job is manual and inconsistent.
•A contractor’s past work and reviews are scattered, so trust is hard to establish.
3

Ideas You Can Solve

Ideas for solving those blockers that are within your ability to solve.

•A marketplace app, Renovation Revolution, connecting homeowners with contractors.
•Structured contractor profiles: services, photos of past jobs, and customer reviews.
•A project posting and bidding flow with pre-contract privacy for the homeowner.
•Photo-based search using image recognition to find the contractor behind a look.
•Data-driven estimate suggestions for labor and parts that improve as data grows.
4

Top 3 Opportunities

The three strongest ideas, each weighed so the trade-offs are clear before committing to a single proposal.

1 Marketplace and bidding flow
Pros
Opens a new revenue stream through transaction or lead fees. Serves both homeowners and contractors, so each side draws in the other. Every later feature (search, reviews, estimates) builds on this foundation, and network effects make it defensible once it has traction.
Cons
Two-sided cold start: it earns nothing until enough of both sides have joined. It is the largest build and the slowest path to revenue, and it needs payment, trust, and safety systems in place before any money can change hands.
2 Photo-based contractor search
Pros
A standout differentiator; helps homeowners find a specific look.
Cons
Needs image-recognition tooling and a library of job photos first.
3 Data-driven estimates
Pros
Long-term ROI; brings contractors back to the app repeatedly.
Cons
Needs many completed transactions before the numbers are trustworthy.
5

ROI & Why It Has Waited

For those same three opportunities, the return on investment and why each has not been done yet.

1 Marketplace and bidding flow
Return on investment
New homeowners on both sides of the marketplace; the foundation every other feature builds on.
Why it has waited
It is the largest build, so it needs to start as an MVP rather than the full vision.
2 Photo-based contractor search
Return on investment
Differentiates us from directories and improves retention.
Why it has waited
Relies on accumulated job photos and image-recognition tooling, so it was not part of the MVP.
3 Data-driven estimates
Return on investment
Higher-quality bids and repeat use; a confidence score that rises as data accumulates.
Why it has waited
Needs transaction volume and data expertise before the estimates are reliable.
6

Choose the Strongest Idea

The strongest idea is a combination of high ROI and feasibility with your current technology and resources.

The idea you will propose
Launch the Renovation Revolution marketplace MVP: contractor profiles, project posting, and bidding.
Why you chose this one
It has the highest ROI and is feasible now with our current stack (REST, a relational database, and object storage for photos). It defers the image-recognition and estimate features until we have the data to support them.
7

Draft the Memo

The content to help you write the memo. Write it addressed to your audience, then move it into your organization's template.

What is the problem?
Homeowners cannot easily find and compare trustworthy contractors, and contractors cannot find relevant projects to bid on. Reputation, past work, and estimates all live in scattered places, which makes both sides slow and uncertain.
What is the solution?
A marketplace app, Renovation Revolution, with structured contractor profiles (services, job photos, reviews) and a project posting and bidding flow with pre-contract privacy for the homeowner. We launch it as an MVP we can expand.
What other solutions were researched but not chosen, and why?
Photo-based image-recognition search was researched but deferred until we have a library of job photos and the ML tooling to support it. Automated labor and parts estimates were also deferred until we have enough completed transactions to produce a useful confidence score.
