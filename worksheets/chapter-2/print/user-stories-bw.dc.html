A user story captures one small piece of what your product does, told from the point of view of the person using it. It keeps the focus on the user and the reason behind a feature, rather than the code. Write a story for each thing a user needs to do, then walk the most important one through its happy path. These stories feed the rest of your design, including the data you will need to store.

The format
As a [type of user], I want to [do something], so that [I get some benefit].

Example: As a returning customer, I want to save my payment details, so that I can check out faster next time.

1

Who Are Your Users?

List every type of person who will use the product. Include people who use it but are not the paying customer.

Examples: a first-time visitor, a returning customer, an account admin, a support agent who uses it on the customer's behalf.
