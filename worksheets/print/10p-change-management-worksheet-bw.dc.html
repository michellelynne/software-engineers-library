Change Management

Think about the application you are working on now. If you were in charge of the change management plan, how would you fill this out? If you are not currently working on one, use the Renovation Revolution case study.

01

Problem Definition

Before anyone will adopt the application, they have to agree there is a problem worth solving. The original memo is a good starting point, but it usually needs rewriting for each audience.

1What problem does this application solve?
2Why did we decide to build it?
3What could we have purchased, and why did we pass?
4What is the impact of not having the application?
5How many people does the problem affect? Which orgs or teams?

What is the expected impact on the existing data at each stage of adoption?

02

Stakeholder Buy-In

If your users do not want to use a new workflow or application, they will not. They will invent work arounds or do the bare minimum. You will need to bring them along and get them invested in the new method.

1What is the current workflow or solution?
2Are there new technical terms everyone needs to learn?
3Expected release timeline
4Potential UAT participants
5How resistant to change is this audience?
6How will you collect feedback?
