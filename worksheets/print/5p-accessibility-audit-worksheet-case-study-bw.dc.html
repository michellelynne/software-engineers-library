Accessibility Audit Worksheet

Renovation Revolution

Automated scanners are a great first pass for accessibility checks, but you should include these checks in your final manual QA review. Automation can tell you whether it includes everything, but only you can say whether the page makes sense to a screen reader. This worksheet includes one section for general setup and a per-page checklist to ensure you don't miss any critical steps.

01

Audit Setup

Requirements depend on where your users are. Write down the standard, the law behind it, and the tools you will actually run, so every page after this is measured the same way.

◇ AI Prompt
"You are a senior frontend engineer responsible for accessibility. Our product is [one sentence on what it does and who uses it], and our users are in [countries or regions]. Tell me which accessibility standards and regulations legally apply to us, name the exact conformance level we have to hit, and list the five requirements at that level that teams most often fail."
02

Accessibility Audit

Fill out one checklist for every page in your app, and then compare pages to catch repetitive issues. For each check, mark Pass if the page is fine, Fix if it needs work, or N/A if it does not apply, then write down what has to change. I have included an example from the case study and then a blank version for you to use.

◇ AI Prompt
"You are a senior frontend engineer reviewing one screen for accessibility. Here is the markup and styling for [page name]: [paste]. Audit it against [insert the rule you need to follow here]. For each failure, give the success criterion number, the exact element, one sentence on why it fails, and a suggested code change. Put the failures a screen reader user would hit first."
