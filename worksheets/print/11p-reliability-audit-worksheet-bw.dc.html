Reliability Audit

After reading about the reliability terms, SLI, SLO, and SLA, we are now going to implement them. If all your applications already have these, review the ones you have for accuracy. Use the Reliability Agreement Examples section to see the case study version. Choose one of your services to start.

Three terms, one dependency chain
SLI (Service Level Indicator) is a metric you measure, such as latency, error rate, or availability.
SLO (Service Level Objective) is the target you set for that indicator, expressed as a percentage over a time window. For example, "99.5% of booking requests will succeed within 3 seconds, measured over a rolling 30-day window."
SLA (Service Level Agreement) is the contract you have with an external customer or another internal team, with consequences attached.
01

The User-Facing Promise

Before writing any numbers, articulate what you are promising the user in plain language. This keeps the promise grounded in experience rather than infrastructure.
02

Define the SLI

The SLI is the specific metric that best captures whether the user experience is good or bad. It should be measurable, user-facing, and as close to the user as possible.

03

SLO Target

The SLO target is the threshold above which you consider the user experience acceptable. It should be ambitious enough to be meaningful but realistic enough that you can meet it consistently.
04

Pressure-Test the Target

These questions are designed to surface assumptions and gaps before you finalize the SLO.
05

Error Budget

The error budget is the allowable amount of failure implied by your SLO. It is what you spend when you take risks such as deployments, experiments, and infrastructure changes, and it is the metric that tells you when to stop taking those risks and make fixes instead.

06

Budget Alerts

Set up alerts that will notify you well before you use up the entire monthly error budget. You want at least two: a fast burn alert for sudden catastrophic failure, and a slow burn alert for gradual degradation that would not trigger a threshold-based alert. It is similar to checking disk capacity. You want to know whether the disk is 95% full, and also whether the write rate means it will be full soon even at 50%.
