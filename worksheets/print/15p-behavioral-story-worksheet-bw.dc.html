Behavioral Story Bank

Start with a recent project, ideally one where you showed leadership or worked at the level you want to be. If the project was chaotic, it's ok to smooth out the edges when you tell it. The interviewer knows they are only hearing the important parts. Fill this out once per project until you have more stories than you think you need.

Why this works
"People don't want to buy a quarter-inch drill. They want a quarter-inch hole."
Theodore Levitt
01

The Project

1Project name
2What problem did you solve? In words anyone at the company would understand.
3What was your role?
4Who did you work with? Use plain titles: manager, teammate, team lead, stakeholder.
5List all the major technology you worked with
02

The Process

1Did you get feedback during the design phase?
2Did it change your solution? Why or why not?
3What were the major milestones?
4What blockers did you have to get past?
5Any issues with collaborators?
6Any major pivots from outside forces? A new tax that changed the math, for example.
7Did you distribute tasks?
8Did you mentor a more junior engineer on it?
03

How It Landed

1What is different today because of what you did?
2Any patents granted, or other major accomplishments?
3What did you learn?
4If you started over fresh, what would you do?
5Draw the line from your work to a goal anyone would recognize

It can take a few hops. "I did x, which affected y, which led to z, and in the end it sold more mugs."

04

Write the Story

Use the details above to write a story where you displayed good judgment. The story needs a clear beginning, middle, end. Then pick another skill to highlight and change the focus of the story. You can talk about different parts of the project to highlight different skills.

1The judgment story
2Which skill is this second story for?
05

Skill Coverage

Here are common skills interviewers are looking for. Write a short note to remind yourself of a story you could tell to highlight each skill. It can relate to the earlier project, but it does not have to. Before a specific interview, redo this using the skills in that organization's values or mission statement.
