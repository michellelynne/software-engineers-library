Find the Right Company

Before you can answer "Why do you want to work here?" in an interview, you need to know what you actually want. There is no single dream job, but you can have a dream industry. This worksheet narrows a huge field of companies down to a focused list that fits your interests, your values, and your hard limits. Work top to bottom, and use AI where prompted to do the heavy research.

01

Your Interests

These might seem obvious, but you'd be surprised what surfaces once you write it down. Getting a job is far easier when you genuinely care about the product.

Hobbies
What do you like to do in your spare time?

Examples: Music, TV, surfing, basketball, board games.

Interests
Which non-fiction subjects, documentaries, shows, or news do you keep coming back to?

Examples: History documentaries, Animal Planet, tech blogs, space travel, marine biology, HGTV real-estate shows.

Passions
What social or societal issues matter to you? What injustice would you change with technology, and for whom?

Examples: Healthcare, immigration, veterans, education, climate change.

02

03

Benefits Filters

Give each benefit an acceptable range.  Salary is the benefit we think about the most, but there are many options. Make sure to compare full packages and negotiate for what you really want. Leave blank anything you don't care about. For example, I want vacation days more than I want equity so I would put in "unlimited" and leave equity blank.  

◇ AI Prompt
"In [region], what is the average range of [benefit] for a senior software engineer with [N] years of experience? What about in [other region]?"
04

Culture Filters

These are the "how" of the work rather than the "what." Add any cultural factor that matters to you and isn't listed.

05

Hard Dealbreakers

Lines you would never cross. Each one should start with "I would never…".  These are non-negotiable, unlike the ranges above.

Examples: "I would never work for a political campaign." · "I would never work fully on-site." · "I would never work in a vice-focused industry."

06

Build Your List & Set Alerts

Apply your top five filters and all your dealbreakers to the company list from Step 2. Aim for a focused list of 25–50 companies, then automate the search so opportunities come to you.

◇ AI Prompt · Filter
"Given this list of companies, which allow remote work? Which have a 401(k) match? Which pay senior engineers more than $200k on average? Make a new list each time so I can see who drops off."
◇ AI Prompt · Alerts
"For these companies, write a Google Alert query for the job equivalent to Senior Software Engineer, matching each company's real title (e.g. L5 at Netflix, L4 at Google), sent as a daily digest. Explain how to set it up."
My target companies (aim for 25–50)
"Why do you want to work here?"

Structure: "I've wanted to work at Company X for a while — I even set a job alert for it. [Why it's on your list.] I've also been looking for [benefits 1, 2 & 3], and learned from my research and network that you offer them."
