Find Your Dream Pretty Good Job

Identifying Industries

This exercise is designed to help you find the most suitable industries. By applying within industries that match your interests and values, you will make it easier to answer questions about why you are there.

You may want to say to me, "Michelle, I don't need to do this, I'm willing to work anywhere." That's not entirely true — everyone has some dealbreakers. For instance, most people don't want to move to Antarctica and get paid $20/hour. That is an extreme example, but by undergoing some introspection, you will discover your priorities.

Getting a job is easier when you are interested in their product. The less experience you have, the more you want to show excitement for the product. Tech is huge. Almost every company uses software in some form. Even an enterprise more grounded in the physical world, like a lawn care company, has to organize its crew, clients, and finances. Then there are the companies for which software/technology is the product, from Google to Salesforce. To narrow down your high-potential industries, let's start with your interests.

01

Identifying Interests

First, let's identify your general interests. These might seem obvious, but you'd be surprised how often you connect a job to something you are interested in by just writing it down.

Which industries are you most interested in?

02

Expand on Interests

For each of the interests you have identified above, answer each of these questions. This task is an excellent opportunity to practice using AI for a non-coding exercise. One thing AI is very good for is market research. It will give you answers you would have never thought of on your own by just brainstorming. You can also have an ongoing conversation to narrow down your interests and then finally create the summary.

Example Interest: Playing Music

I have two different options here. When I first wrote this chapter, I was doing this type of research step by step. As AIs have already gotten so much better, you can take a shortcut and go with a simple prompt. I have included both options for you to try.

◇ AI Prompt · Shortcut
"I like music. Create a list of companies I might like to work at."

Multi-Step Research

What are some B2B (business-to-business) companies (or categories thereof) that align with one of your key interests?

Examples: Music Distribution, Nielsen Ratings for Radio

◇ AI Prompt
"Give me a list of companies in the music industry that are B2B."
What are some B2C (business-to-consumer) companies (or categories thereof) that align with one of your key interests?

Examples: Pandora, Spotify

◇ AI Prompt
"Give me a list of companies in the music industry that are B2C."
In what ways do companies provide education in that space?

Examples: Apps to Learn an Instrument, Tools for Music Teachers

◇ AI Prompt
"Give me a list of companies in the music education industry."
What type of companies supply in that category?

Examples: Instruments, Speakers, Ticket Sales, (Fender, Klipsch, LiveNation)

◇ AI Prompt
"Give me a list of companies in the music industry that provide a physical item. Give me a list of companies that are vendors or suppliers for a company in the music industry."
What type of companies provide retail or e-commerce in that category?

Examples: Bandcamp, Guitar Store

◇ AI Prompt
"Give me a list of retail companies in the music industry."

This table is going to be very large. That is good because it helps you change your career goal from "dream company" to "dream industry". If you only want to work at a single company, you are setting yourself up for failure. I never got to work at Pixar. But, by enlarging that to "Entertainment Industry," it was a lot easier to find a job. After the table is filled in, look over the companies. Which of them seems the most interesting at a glance? Can you pull out the top hundred? It might be helpful to sort the interests by the most interesting first, or you might realize you want the education companies from all the interests. These are more like gut feelings than data-driven. How do the companies make you feel? The following two worksheets will help you gather the data to create a data-driven decision.
