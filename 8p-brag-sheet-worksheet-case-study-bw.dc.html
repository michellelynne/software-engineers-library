Brag Sheet

Renovation Revolution

Brag Sheets aren’t as common as resumes, but they should be. They are useful for performance reviews, promotions, and introductions to new managers, or anyone who is curious about what you’ve been up to. Brag Sheets differ from resumes in that they look to the future and not just the past. They include what you’ve done, what you want to do, and how you’ll get there.

The examples come from Renovation Revolution, written from the point of view of a mid-level engineer on the vendor integrations team. Update your own sheet once a quarter, right after you refresh your Success Statements.

AI Prompt
“You are an engineering manager who is reviewing documentation for raises and promotions for your team. Here are my Success Statements from the last four quarters: [paste]. Group them into no more than three records of achievement, each one to two sentences. Choose the most impressive, written to show I should get the maximum available raise. Based on my current role and progress, suggest goals I should strive for next, either within my organization or in the tech community. For each of those goals, suggest concrete steps I can take to achieve them.”

◆“I opened Renovation Revolution to a new class of supplier, growing the catalog 18% and producing $10,000 in pilot sales in a single quarter.”
◆“I cut customer churn on plumbing products 10% by making the product data on the page trustworthy.”

Goals

These are your career goals. They can be both near- and long-term. They can include both in the organization and in the community. Organizational goals can include leading a project, being promoted to a senior role, or expanding into fullstack work. Community goals could include contributing to an open-source project, speaking at a conference, or expanding your knowledge by attending one.

Example
◆Promotion to Senior Software Engineer, leading the next vendor integration end to end.
◆Own the vendor integration platform rather than one-off connectors.
◆Give a conference talk on integrating legacy vendor catalogs.

Progress Monitor

These are the concrete steps you plan to take to achieve your goals. They can include tasks such as taking a class, volunteering for a specific project, or reading a super cool book (ahem). Whether you haven’t started or you’re almost done, you should indicate your progress. Each item here should be connected to a goal, and you should periodically update this section as you make progress.

Example
◆Wrote the technical design for the vendor catalog sync (promotion to senior): done, reviewed by two senior engineers
◆Mentored a new hire through their first three merged pull requests (promotion to senior): done
◆Drafting a proposal for a shared connector framework (own the platform): in progress, targeting the next planning cycle
◆Submit a talk to two conferences (conference talk): not started, CFPs open in March
