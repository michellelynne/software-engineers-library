# SKILL: Regional Communication Adapter

**From:** Influencing Leadership (Other Cultures)

## When to load this skill

Load this skill when the reader has something to ask their manager and wants to know how it
will land. Also load it when they work across regions, report to someone in another country, or
have had an ask go badly in a way they did not understand.

## What this adapts against

Four dimensions, which is what the reader is being asked to account for:

- **Directness.** Whether an ask is stated plainly or approached.
- **Formality.** Register, channel, and how much ceremony a request carries.
- **Hierarchy.** How much distance the reader is expected to observe, and whether going to a
  manager directly is normal or presumptuous.
- **Timing.** When an ask is raised relative to review cycles, and how much notice is expected.

## How to run it

**Step 1. Get the actual draft.** The words the reader plans to use, not a summary of the
intent. The whole point is how it is phrased.

**Step 2. Get the region,** and separately, anything they know about the company's own culture.
These are two different things and the second one often overrides the first. A US company's
office abroad may run on the parent culture, the local one, or an uneasy mixture.

**Step 3. Explain how it would likely land,** walking the four dimensions and saying which
matter most for this particular ask.

**Step 4. Rewrite it** so it lands well locally, preserving what the reader actually wants.
An adaptation that quietly waters down the request has failed.

**Step 5. Flag the extremes.** Anything that would read as too aggressive, and anything that
would read as too passive to be taken as a real request. Both are failure modes, and which one
a reader tends toward is usually invisible to them.

**Step 6. Address the channel.** How the ask is made, whether in person, by phone, or in
writing, can matter as much as the words. Say which fits this ask in this context.

## Prompt the reader can run directly

> You are a career coach who has managed and promoted software engineers in [REGION] for many
> years. I am a mid-level software engineer working toward a promotion to Senior. Here is what
> I plan to say to my manager: "[YOUR DRAFT ASK]".
>
> Tell me how a manager in [REGION] would likely receive it, which cultural expectations I
> should account for (directness, formality, hierarchy, and timing), and rewrite the ask so it
> lands well there. Flag anything that would read as too aggressive or too passive locally.

Add, if the company culture differs from the local one:

> My company is headquartered in [COUNTRY] and the office culture is [WHAT YOU HAVE OBSERVED].
> Tell me where that pulls against the local norms and which one to follow for this ask.

## What to tell the reader

- **These are generalizations, and your manager is a person.** Regional patterns describe
  tendencies across many people and predict no individual reliably. Treat the output as a
  hypothesis to check against the manager the reader actually has, not a rule about a country.
  This caution is an addition here rather than something the book states.
- **A human coach is better.** Someone familiar with the specific region and, ideally, the
  specific company will beat any general answer. This is the fallback, not the first choice.
- **Company culture may outrank regional culture.** Learn the spoken and unspoken rules of the
  organization before making views known.
- **Adjust for yourself too.** The reader's own cultural defaults are shaping the draft in ways
  they cannot see, which is half the reason a second read is useful.

## What this skill does not do

It does not make the ask, predict the outcome, or know the reader's manager. It gives a
considered second read on how a request might be heard somewhere the reader has not worked.
