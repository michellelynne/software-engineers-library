Distributed Systems Design

We’re going to design the distributed systems for the Renovation Revolution case study. We are very confident in our product and believe it will eventually have millions of users. So what services do we need? In Figure 4.5 in Chapter 4, you can see where I started adding some nodes. There is the control plane service, the notification service, and the image review service. Now let’s focus on a single workflow.

What are all the services that the case study application can use, and how will they communicate with each other? What messages do they send to each other? Do they communicate one-way or talk back-and-forth? In Figure 9.6, you can only see part of the system. Designing the entire system can feel daunting, so breaking it into workflows can make it more manageable and easier to read.

I have filled in one of the more complex workflows. Can you complete the others? A single service can be used in multiple workflows, so feel free to reuse any services from the example. I like to keep my initial designs simple, but you can use any design that communicates the different services and the data shared between them. The notes are optional, but can help you clarify your thoughts. This is also great practice for system design interviews, which we’ll discuss in greater detail in Chapter 16.

Before you sketch
If your organization has a visual standard for flowcharts, use it. There are design standards, such as the C4 model, as well as architecture guides for each cloud provider, including AWS, Microsoft, and Google. You can find the links to them in the Further Reading section at the end of the book.
