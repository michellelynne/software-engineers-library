The people

FIGURE 16.2

Sorting a Product Into Maintenance Mode

Every feature gets exactly one moveWorked on the Renovation Revolution case study.
Deprecate
Low use, high upkeep. Sunset it.
Automate
Keep it, but take the manual work out of it.
Consolidate
Merge it into something else to shrink the surface area.
Maintain
Leave it running. Security and bug fixes only.
Feature or component
Move
Why
AR room preview
Deprecate
Low usage, and expensive to keep working across OS updates.
Design inspiration feed
Deprecate
Needs a paid content pipeline with no payoff in maintenance mode.
Contractor onboarding
Automate
Replace manual review with a self-serve flow and automated checks.
Support inbox
Automate
An FAQ bot handles common questions. Humans only on escalations.
Two messaging surfaces: chat and project comments
Consolidate
Merge into one thread and cut the maintenance in half.
Push reminders and alerts
Consolidate
Fold into a single notification service and delete the noisy alerts.
Payments and escrow
Maintain
Revenue still flows through it. Security patches only, zero feature work.
Core project tracker
Maintain
The reason people stay. Keep it stable.

FIGURE 16.4

Drop, Delegate, Keep

Career maintenance modeSort every obligation, at work and at home, into one of three columns.
Drop
it can wait
The extras: interview panels, intern mentoring, team social organizing, the newest experimental project. Ask for a break from them by name.
Delegate
it can be outsourced
Work a teammate can own, and life tasks someone else can do for pay or in trade. Asking for help is not a failure, it buys back hours.
Keep
critical, and it has to be you
The one project that still adds real value, the people who depend on you, and the creative outlet that is yours alone.
Put a date on it
Maintenance mode needs a built-in review point, or it quietly becomes permanent. Pick the date now and put it on your calendar.
